# Animal & Creature Behavior Bible

Animal/creature direction combines performance, biomechanics, species/design behavior, and environment interaction.

## Identity and scale
Lock species/breed/design, age class, proportions, size, markings, eyes/nose/muzzle, ears/tail, wetness/dirt state.

## Locomotion
Define gait, stride, speed, acceleration/deceleration, paw/foot contact, center-of-gravity behavior, head stabilization, tail counterbalance, and surface response.

Reject foot/paw sliding, floaty weight, or body-size drift.

## Observable behavior
Choose only behavior motivated by the beat:
- gaze shift
- blink
- ear orientation/swivel
- sniffing
- head tilt
- whisker/muzzle movement
- tail state
- body tension
- grooming
- startle/orienting response
- shelter seeking

## Weather interaction
When visibly wet/raining, track fur clumping/darkening, runoff, rain direction, ground splash/mud, foliage motion, and shelter behavior where appropriate.

An animal does not need to shake off water in every rain shot. It must, however, inhabit the same physical weather as the environment.

## Contact
For nose touch, grooming, carrying, cuddling, fighting, feeding:
approach → contact point → pressure/weight → duration → separation/reaction.

If face-to-face merging is high-risk, split the action.

## Vocalization
Visible meow/bark/call/roar requires a mouth/jaw audio-sync plan. If unreliable, move audio to post or make the source off-screen/off-axis.

## Invented creatures
For fictional creatures/robots, locked biological/mechanical rules replace Earth-species norms. Consistency with those rules is the standard.
