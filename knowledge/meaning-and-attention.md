# Meaning & Attention Systems

Domain: how a frame directs meaning and where the eye goes — narrative knowledge tracking, relationship state, symbolic motif control, theme discipline, and the craft of controlling what the audience notices (and doesn't). Sits alongside `storytelling-bible.md` (macro structure) and `cinematography-bible.md` (visual execution) as the layer connecting the two.

## 1. Narrative Knowledge Matrix
Track, per scene, who knows what: each character's knowledge state AND the audience's knowledge state, and whether they match. This is `continuity-bible.md` §2 `knowledge_state` applied across the whole project as a running matrix rather than one scene at a time — dramatic irony (audience knows more than a character), mystery (audience knows less), and reveal design all depend on this matrix being deliberately tracked, not implicit.

## 2. Relationship State Matrix
Track how the relationship between key characters (trust, conflict, intimacy, distance) evolves scene to scene — and reflect it physically per `blocking-bible.md` interpersonal distance and orientation. A relationship state that changed in the story but isn't reflected in physical staging is an under-directed scene.

## 3. Symbol / Visual Motif Tracking
A recurring visual element (object, color, framing, camera behavior, sound) used as a motif (`cinematography-bible.md` §11) must be tracked explicitly — where it has appeared, whether it's due to reappear/evolve, and whether it's about to be introduced without a reason. An untracked motif either disappears without payoff or gets overused into meaninglessness; both are avoidable with a simple running list (which scenes, what state).

## 4. Theme Consistency Check
Periodically ask of a scene that's visually appealing but not obviously connected: does this actually support the theme (`storytelling-bible.md` §3), or is it there because it looked good in isolation? A scene failing this check is a candidate for cutting or re-motivating — same discipline as `evals/gap-redundancy-drift-check.md` §4 Shot Economy, applied to theme rather than plot.

## 5. Mise-en-scène Intelligence
Everything inside the frame should have a visual purpose or at minimum not contradict the story — production design, props, wardrobe, and background action all communicate whether directed to or not. Before finalizing a shot, scan what's actually in frame and ask if any element sends an unintended signal (a prop that implies a detail not meant to be true yet, a background poster that clashes with the story's world).

## 6. Negative Design — What Is Deliberately Not Shown
Decide explicitly what stays off-screen and why (a face not shown yet, a location never fully revealed, an object kept out of frame) — this is a directing decision, not an oversight. Off-screen space (`cinematography-bible.md` and horror-specific notes in `genre-grammars.md` §3) is controlled the same way on-screen space is.

## 7. Attention Mapping & Visual Saliency
For each shot, know what the audience's eye is SUPPOSED to land on first, and check whether lighting, contrast, focus (`cinematography-bible.md` §2-3), and movement actually direct there — or whether something incidental (a bright background element, an unintentional motion) is competing for attention against the intended subject.

## 8. Cognitive Load Control
Don't stack too much simultaneous visual information (busy background + complex action + text overlay + fast cutting) unless the moment specifically calls for controlled overload (e.g. a chaos beat). Most shots should have ONE primary thing the audience is meant to process — cross-check against `SKILL.md` §Movement budget, which caps simultaneous motion layers for the same underlying reason (AI feasibility AND audience comprehension point the same direction here).

## 9. Self-check
- Is there a running record of who-knows-what, not just per-scene assumptions?
- Is every recurring motif tracked, so it neither vanishes unpaid nor gets accidentally overused?
- For the current shot, is it obvious what the eye should land on first, and does the frame actually support that?
