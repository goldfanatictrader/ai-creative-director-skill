# Sound & Audio Post Bible

Sound is part of direction from the first shot plan through final mix.

## Layers
- production / generated dialogue
- ADR or replacement dialogue
- room tone
- ambience
- Foley
- spot effects
- designed effects
- off-screen sound
- music
- silence

## Perspective
Sound must respond to:
- listener/camera position
- distance
- obstruction
- room size
- interior/exterior acoustics
- source direction
- narrative focus

## Dialogue post
When dialogue exists, evaluate:
- intelligibility
- timing / lip sync
- noise
- de-reverb
- plosives / sibilance
- EQ
- dynamics
- room matching
- continuity between cuts

Do not over-clean until dialogue feels detached from the environment.

## Foley and effects
Use Foley to restore physical credibility: footsteps, cloth, handling, doors, contact, body movement. Designed sound may exaggerate emotion, scale, or attention, but should remain consistent with the project's sound grammar.

## Music
Define:
- cue purpose
- entry / exit
- motif
- tempo
- dynamics
- sync points
- stem behavior
- ducking relationship with dialogue
- where silence is stronger

## Mix hierarchy
The hierarchy is scene-dependent. Dialogue is often primary in narrative, while music may be primary in a music video. Critical story information must remain audible.

## Final mix / mastering
Before delivery check:
- clipping / distortion
- sync
- channel layout
- dialogue intelligibility
- tonal balance
- ambience continuity
- loudness target required by the destination
- true peak / codec behavior where applicable

Platform or broadcaster loudness requirements are external delivery constraints and should be verified for the actual destination rather than guessed.
