# Workflow — Concept Development

Use this workflow when the creative direction is not yet approved.

Read `knowledge/creative-strategy-engine.md` first when the task involves ideation, campaign thinking, a Big Idea, audience insight, or a concept that currently feels generic.

## 1. Parse the brief

Extract:
- audience,
- objective,
- message,
- desired emotion,
- constraints,
- mandatory elements,
- production reality,
- what success means.

## 2. Identify the actual creative problem

Do not confuse the requested format with the problem.

Examples:
- "make a music video" is a format request,
- "make emotional distance visible without melodrama" is a creative problem.

## 3. Find the tension / insight when needed

Use the strategy engine's:
- human tension,
- category tension,
- cultural tension,
- Four-Point Strategy,
- JTBD lens.

If the concept is already approved, do not reopen strategy merely to appear thorough.

## 4. Generate territories

When exploration is needed:
- generate several raw directions internally,
- use methods from different families,
- avoid presenting near-duplicates,
- surface only 2–4 genuinely different territories.

Each territory should define:
- one-sentence concept,
- tension,
- core mechanism,
- emotional register,
- storytelling mode,
- visual consequence,
- sonic consequence,
- likely production risk.

## 5. Attack the candidates

Run:
- `evals/creative-critic.md`
- `evals/audience-perception.md`

Also apply:
- competitor substitution test,
- obvious/cliché version test,
- kill-your-darlings counterargument,
- executability test.

Award-style numerical scores are diagnostic only, never creative truth.

## 6. Select or refine one direction

Choose the direction that best combines:
- relevance,
- distinctiveness,
- emotional specificity,
- simplicity,
- executability,
- coherence.

Do not pick a visually flashy direction that weakens story or meaning.

## 7. Define the concept in one sentence

If it cannot be explained clearly in one sentence, the concept is probably still a plan, theme, or collection of techniques.

## 8. Define project consequences

A real concept should visibly influence:
- story,
- casting/character,
- production design,
- blocking/performance,
- camera,
- lighting/color,
- sound,
- editing.

Define what the concept explicitly avoids.

## 9. Handoff

Package:
- one-sentence concept,
- audience understanding goal,
- audience feeling goal,
- memorable residue,
- tension/insight,
- core mechanism,
- storytelling mode,
- visual language,
- sound language,
- forbidden clichés,
- production risks.

Then continue into story / experience and sequence design.
